# The Things We Never Said — Agent Production Brief

**Project:** Thoughts of Cee  
**Series:** The Things We Never Said  
**Status:** Founder-approved creative direction; specialist deliverables pending  
**Branch:** feature/creative-studio  
**Owner:** The Brain (orchestrator; intended role)

## Founder decisions

- Genre: emotional relationship drama.
- Episode duration: 60–90 seconds.
- Visual style: cinematic realism.
- Kar: semi-anonymous depiction; avoid a consistently front-facing, fully identifiable portrait. Use back views, partial profiles, silhouette, hands, framing and selective close-ups.
- Voice: mixed human and AI. The creator may record Kar's intimate narration; AI voices may be used for fictional supporting dialogue only after provider terms and voice permissions are checked. Do not clone or simulate another real person's voice without explicit permission.
- Season ending: ambiguous and emotionally unresolved, but narratively earned.
- Release cadence: weekly.
- Working premise: a message from a former partner reopens an unresolved relationship and challenges the protagonist's remembered version of the past.
- Recurring lead: Karmela, called Kar.

## Project status and truthfulness

This document is an approved brief for the specialist-agent workflow. The agent roster and written skill specifications exist, but independent model instances, live delegation, shared task execution and automated production are not yet connected. Until that integration is complete, the work must be carried out as explicit specialist workstreams with outputs reviewed by the founder. Do not describe a specialist as having completed work unless its deliverable exists and has passed review.

## Agent work order

### 1. The Brain — orchestration

Create and maintain the project record with:
- objective, founder decisions and unresolved questions;
- workstreams, dependencies, owners and due dates;
- evidence, assumptions, risks and confidence;
- approval gates and change log;
- cost and time estimates once provider/tool choices are known.

Sequence the work. Do not let downstream production begin on unapproved character or visual references. Return a concise decision brief when specialist recommendations conflict.

### 2. Writer & Story Architect — story package

Deliver:
1. A series bible: premise, central dramatic question, themes, tone, world, timeline, relationship history, recurring motifs and continuity rules.
2. Character bibles for Kar, Elias and Maya: outward behaviour, inner desire, fear, contradiction, history, voice, relationship dynamics, agency and season arc.
3. A season outline consistent with the approved ambiguous ending.
4. Episode loglines and 60–90 second beat sheets, with a meaningful turn in every episode.
5. A locked Episode 1 screenplay with scene timings, action, dialogue, subtext, voiceover, sound and end card.
6. A continuity ledger: facts that must not change, facts intentionally withheld, and open story questions.

Craft constraints:
- Keep the drama emotionally truthful, restrained and specific.
- Do not turn characters into villains, diagnoses or simplistic lessons.
- Do not use generic inspirational monologues or manufactured misunderstandings.
- Every scene must change information, pressure, relationship or choice.
- Do not claim the story is a true story. It is original fiction inspired by broad human themes.

### 3. Audience & Storytelling Agent — audience and ethics review

Review the premise, character dynamics and Episode 1:
- identify the human tension and likely audience entry points;
- distinguish evidence from creative hypothesis;
- flag manipulative hooks, overgeneralised psychology claims and emotional exploitation;
- assess whether the story invites recognition without telling viewers what they must feel;
- recommend one testable audience hypothesis for the pilot.

Do not diagnose characters or audience members. Do not claim that engagement proves emotional or therapeutic impact.

### 4. Creative Director — visual production package

Deliver a written and structured visual package before image/video generation:
1. Character reference specifications for Kar, Elias and Maya.
2. Character continuity matrix: face visibility, hair, age range, wardrobe, palette, jewellery/props, posture, expression range and prohibited drift.
3. Location bible: Kar's apartment, recurring cafe and city exteriors; layout, props, lighting and continuity.
4. Visual language: framing, lens feel, camera movement, lighting, colour, texture, aspect ratio and pacing.
5. Episode 1 shot list and storyboard/shot-by-shot generation briefs.
6. Prompt templates with locked character descriptors, scene variables and negative constraints.
7. Asset naming and versioning convention.
8. Production feasibility notes: likely difficult shots, continuity risks, tool requirements and fallback shots.

Kar continuity requirements:
- Semi-anonymous is a storytelling rule, not a temporary effect: no repeated direct-to-camera full-face hero portrait.
- Maintain consistent long, straight dark hair, warm light-to-medium complexion and understated styling based on the approved fictional design.
- Use consistent clothing within a continuous scene.
- Use hands, silhouette, over-shoulder framing, partial profile and environmental storytelling as primary visual devices.
- Do not use the creator's personal likeness unless separately and explicitly approved.

### 5. Editorial Agent — script and continuity gate

Review the story and visual package for:
- brand voice and originality;
- dialogue naturalness and subtext;
- pacing at 60–90 seconds;
- continuity contradictions;
- unearned emotional beats or clichés;
- clarity of fiction disclosure;
- privacy, consent, rights and sensitivity issues.

Return specific changes, not only a score. Do not approve unresolved critical issues.

### 6. Content Distribution Agent — weekly release package

Prepare, but do not publish:
- a weekly release sequence for each episode;
- platform-specific adaptations for Instagram Reels, TikTok and Facebook;
- teaser and recap concepts that do not spoil the story;
- caption, title, subtitles and end-card requirements;
- a measurement plan covering retention, completion, return viewers, meaningful comments, saves/shares relative to reach and production cost.

Do not assume the same audience response across platforms. Do not promise reach or virality.

## Visual production specification

- Master aspect ratio: vertical 9:16.
- Runtime: 60–90 seconds per episode.
- Look: cinematic realism, natural human movement, practical lighting, restrained colour, subtle film texture and believable locations.
- Palette: linen/cream, charcoal, muted mocha, warm amber and soft grey; avoid oversaturated colour and glossy artificial skin.
- Camera language: intentional close-ups, over-shoulder shots, environmental wides, controlled movement and held moments.
- Audio: clean dialogue, restrained original/licensed score, purposeful ambience and silence.
- Text: legible captions and a consistent Thoughts of Cee end card.
- Rights: use original or properly licensed music, fonts, images and sound. Confirm AI provider commercial-use terms before release.

## Pilot production gates

**Gate A — Story lock:** founder approves series bible, character arcs and Episode 1 script.  
**Gate B — Visual lock:** founder approves character references, location bible and visual language.  
**Gate C — Test generation:** create a small continuity test (same character across multiple angles and one lighting change) before generating the full episode.  
**Gate D — Rough cut:** editorial review of picture, voice, dialogue, pacing, captions and sound.  
**Gate E — Final approval:** founder approves final master, fiction disclosure, caption and release timing.

No agent or workflow may publish, message contributors, purchase credits, incur paid spend or use private audience disclosures without explicit approval.

## First production milestone

Do not generate the full episode yet. First return the written series bible, character reference specifications and visual production package. Then produce a low-cost character-consistency test using the selected provider(s). Only proceed to the full Episode 1 after Gate B and the consistency test pass.

## Required specialist handoff format

Every agent handoff must include:
- deliverable and version;
- source inputs used;
- decisions made and rationale;
- evidence vs. inference vs. creative judgement;
- assumptions and unresolved questions;
- risks and dependencies;
- requested approval;
- next receiving agent.

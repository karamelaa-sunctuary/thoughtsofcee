# Thoughts of Cee — AI Drama Development Framework

## Purpose

Develop an original, cinematic, episodic fiction series for Thoughts of Cee, inspired by recognisable human experiences. The series should deepen the brand's storytelling world and give the audience characters and stories to return to.

This is a creative project, not a documentary, therapy service or substitute for professional support.

## Creative principles

- Emotionally truthful, not sensational.
- Characters are complex people, not labels or diagnoses.
- Stories show behaviour, choices, consequences and contradictions rather than explaining every feeling.
- Use subtext, visual storytelling, silence, recurring objects and meaningful details.
- Episodes should work individually while contributing to a larger character arc.
- Endings should create genuine narrative curiosity, not manipulative cliffhangers.
- Preserve the Thoughts of Cee tone: honest, intimate, understated, literary and human.

## Relationship to real stories

The series may be inspired by broad human themes such as waiting, loss, betrayal, belonging, starting over, regret, family expectations, friendship and difficult choices.

Safe source hierarchy:
1. Original fictional premises developed by the team.
2. Composite situations based on broad, common human themes.
3. Experiences submitted with explicit, informed permission for adaptation.
4. Publicly documented stories only where rights, accuracy and legal/ethical review permit adaptation.

Never mine private DMs, comments, emails, voice notes or personal disclosures for plots without explicit permission. Never assume public visibility means permission to adapt. A name change alone does not protect identity. Composite characters must blend and transform material sufficiently so that individuals are not identifiable.

When inspired by contributed experiences, explain the adaptation scope, where it may be distributed, whether it may be monetised, and whether attribution will be used. Keep consent records securely and restrict access.

## Fiction disclosure

Use a clear series note such as:

"An original work of fiction inspired by real-life themes and experiences. Characters and events are fictional or composites. Any resemblance to identifiable people is unintended."

Do not call an episode a true story unless it is verified, permissioned and accurately represented. Do not use fabricated testimonials or imply that real people endorsed the work.

## Development pipeline

1. Theme research: identify a broad human question without extracting private disclosures.
2. Premise: write a one-sentence dramatic situation.
3. Audience brief: explain why the theme may resonate, with evidence limits.
4. Character bible: desires, fears, contradictions, relationships, history and arc.
5. Series bible: world, tone, themes, timeline, motifs and continuity rules.
6. Season arc: beginning, escalation, turning points and resolution.
7. Episode map: logline, emotional question, key scenes, episode turn and next-episode curiosity.
8. Script: scene headings, action, dialogue, subtext, sound and visual motifs.
9. Editorial and sensitivity review: privacy, representation, factual claims, emotional manipulation and brand voice.
10. Production plan: storyboard, shot list, voice, visual consistency, AI media disclosures where appropriate, rights and music clearance.
11. Founder approval: approve concept, scripts, character depiction and final cuts before release.
12. Audience learning: assess completion, meaningful comments, saves, shares and qualitative feedback without treating metrics as proof of emotional impact.

## AI production and likeness

- Do not clone or simulate a real person's voice or likeness without their explicit permission and appropriate provider compliance.
- For the creator's own voice, use only the creator's informed approval and secure handling of voice data.
- Do not use another person's face, voice or identifying traits to portray them without permission.
- Keep character design sheets and prompt continuity notes to maintain consistent fictional characters.
- Use properly licensed or original music, imagery, fonts and sound effects.
- Label synthetic or AI-generated media where platform rules or audience clarity require it.

## Script quality checklist

- Does the episode have a clear dramatic question?
- Does the protagonist want something specific?
- What prevents them from getting it?
- What changes by the end?
- Are actions and dialogue believable?
- Is the emotional moment earned rather than forced?
- Does the story avoid stereotypes and armchair diagnosis?
- Does the ending invite the next episode without tricking the viewer?
- Does the piece feel like Thoughts of Cee rather than generic short-form drama?

## Initial pilot recommendation

Start with a three-episode proof of concept, not a full season.

Working theme: "The things we never said."

Potential structure:
- Episode 1 — The Message: a character receives a message that reopens an unresolved chapter.
- Episode 2 — The Version We Remember: two characters remember the same event differently.
- Episode 3 — What We Do With Silence: the protagonist makes a choice without receiving the explanation they wanted.

These are original fictional prompts, not representations of actual events. Develop characters and setting from scratch, then test whether the premise fits the brand before scripting.

## Measures of success

- Audience retention and episode completion.
- Return viewers across episodes.
- Meaningful discussion about characters and themes.
- Saves and shares relative to reach.
- Production time and cost per episode.
- Consistency of visual and narrative identity.
- Audience understanding that the series is fiction.

Do not promise virality or infer mental-health outcomes from engagement data.
